# The two campaigns, and the relationship they both run through

**31 July 2026.** A strategic note. Nobody has named this tension out loud, and drifting into it costs more than deciding it.

---

## The situation

Two campaigns are running at once, and they look independent:

1. **The job search.** Seven applications built for Riot, Sony, VRChat, Netflix and Roblox. Target: a senior in-house seat at a marquee employer, $190K–$1M, relocation now open.
2. **The venture.** Dividual — one delivered study, zero revenue, an outreach kit and a target list of named people at named companies.

They are not independent. **They run through the same relationship**, and that relationship can only be spent once.

## The hinge

Ahmad Azadvar is simultaneously:

- **The venture's only live client contact.** He commissioned the data, received the BO7 study, and the last exchange put a fragility-nudge test up for a this-quarter slot. Converting that engagement to paid is the single highest-value action the venture has.
- **The job search's warmest path.** He is Lead Quantitative User Researcher at Activision, a former Ubisoft Massive colleague (documented — the June 2016 team roster deck lists you both), and Blizzard's **Global Insights** team posted a req two days ago, which means that org is hiring and budgeted.

**Asking him for both is the mistake to avoid.** A vendor who is also job-hunting reads as someone using the engagement as a foot in the door — which retroactively cheapens the unpaid work. A candidate who is also selling reads as someone who does not really want the job. Each ask weakens the other, and you get one clean shot at framing.

## The choice, stated plainly

**Path A — treat him as a client.** Ask to convert the study to a paid engagement. Upside: revenue, a named reference, and the venture stops being pre-revenue. Downside: you forgo the referral into an org that is visibly hiring, and consultancy income at this stage is lumpy and slow.

**Path B — treat him as a referrer.** Ask what is opening inside Global Insights. Upside: a warm internal path at a company where your work is already in use, into a salaried seat with benefits — which matters more now than it did last week. Downside: you convert a commercial relationship into a personal favour, and the unpaid study becomes something you gave away.

**Path C — sequence them.** Ask about the paid conversion first. If it converts, the venture has a client and you can raise the employment question later from a position of strength — a vendor who is also a candidate reads very differently once money has changed hands. If it does not convert within a defined window, the relationship has told you what it is, and you switch to Path B without having wasted the ask.

**Path C is the recommendation.** It costs one conversation's delay and keeps both options alive. It also resolves in your favour either way: a paying client is a better CV line than a referral, and a failed conversion is a clean signal to pivot.

## Two constraints that bear on this

**The unpaid study is unprotected.** No contract, no NDA. That is a reason to move on the paid conversion soon: right now you have delivered real work with no instrument recording that you did. A signed agreement fixes both the revenue and the provenance.

**A Riot offer creates a conflict worth anticipating.** You have read a live-service FPS publisher's telemetry. If Riot — a direct competitor — makes an offer, expect a question about it, and the honest answer is straightforward: the engagement was unpaid, pre-contract, and you hold no client data. Better to have that answer ready than to improvise it.

## What this means for the near term

- **Do not send both asks in the same week.** Whichever goes first sets the frame.
- **The paid conversion is time-sensitive in a way the referral is not.** A study delivered in July is fresh in August and stale in October.
- **The Global Insights req is a signal, not an opportunity** — it is a program-management role you would be mis-slotted into. Its value is that it proves the org is budgeted. That signal keeps for a few weeks.

## The honest bottom line

The venture has done real work and been paid nothing. The job search has produced two rejections and no interviews. Neither is failing; both are early. But they are competing for one relationship and for the same hours, and the pregnancy shortens the runway on deciding which one is the plan and which is the hedge.

That decision does not have to be made today. It should be made deliberately, and written down here when it is.
